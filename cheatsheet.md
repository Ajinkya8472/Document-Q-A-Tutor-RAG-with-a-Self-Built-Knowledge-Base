## Gen-AI Knowledge-Base Tutor (RAG), PS 79

## One-line pitch
A tutor that answers questions about a **self-built Gen-AI knowledge base (97 documents)** or about any document you upload, **only from those documents**, with source citations, and that **refuses** when the answer is not there. It runs a local open LLM (Qwen2.5-1.5B) on a laptop GPU and is deployed through a public link.

## Pipeline (say it in this order)
1. **Ingest:** PDF/TXT -> sentence-aware chunks (~110 words, 1-sentence overlap); each chunk keeps its document and page.
2. **Retrieve:** MiniLM embeddings fused with TF-IDF keyword scores, top-3 chunks.
3. **Gate 1:** best similarity below 0.25 -> refuse.
4. **Generate:** Qwen2.5-1.5B answers from the chunks only, or says NOT_IN_CHAPTER (**Gate 2**).
5. **Attribute:** if the model did not cite, the system attaches the passage that best matches the answer.
6. **Grounding score:** share of answer words found in the retrieved text.

## Our own dataset (what we built)
- **Corpus:** 97 documents, about 460,000 words, 4,913 chunks. 95 Wikipedia articles on Gen-AI topics (CC BY-SA) plus our own papers.
- **Q&A set:** 398 answerable questions, generated by Qwen from corpus chunks and filtered for grounding. Each is tied to its source chunk, so retrieval can be scored.
- **Quality check:** we reviewed 50 by hand: 48 good (96%). Meaning: the answer is supported by the passage, not that every question is perfectly worded.
- **Unanswerable set:** 40 questions: 20 near-topic with made-up names (e.g. "Zorvath attention mechanism") and 20 off-topic.
- Be upfront: the Wikipedia text is not ours; the curation, Q&A set, review and benchmark are.

## Final results (398 answerable + 40 unanswerable questions)
| Metric | No LLM (copies sentences) | **Qwen2.5-1.5B (final)** |
|---|---|---|
| Answer accuracy (key facts) | 75% | **82%** |
| Accuracy of questions it chose to answer | n/a | **88%** (327/372) |
| Reference-answer word recall | 0.60 | **0.72** |
| Correct refusals | 20% (8/40) | **92% (37/40)** |
| Hallucination rate | 80% | **8% (3/40)** |
| Valid questions wrongly refused | 0% | 7% (26/398) |
| Citations | copied text | 99% attached; **66% point to the true source passage** |

**Retrieval (same in both runs):** gold chunk found at rank 1: 60%; in top 3: **79%**; in top 5: 84%; MRR@5 0.70; right *document* in top 3: **92%** (out of 4,913 chunks).

**Model and tokens:** 1,543.7 M parameters, 3 GB in fp16, 32,768-token context; embedder MiniLM 22.7 M parameters. Prompt 509 tokens on average (max 862), answer 68 tokens (max 200), about 1.6% of the context window.

**Speed (be honest about the range):** 1.9 s per answer and 36.5 tokens/s on an idle laptop; 4.9 s and 13.8 tokens/s when the laptop was under load (p95 4.1 s / 12 s). Retrieval is 8 to 21 ms.

## Failure analysis (shows depth: say these before they ask)
- **Retrieval is the bottleneck:** accuracy is 91% when the right chunk is retrieved, but 49% when it is missed.
- **All 26 wrongly refused questions were refused by the LLM**, none by the similarity gate. Several of them are vaguely generated questions that do not name their subject ("What preprocessing steps were taken...?"), so refusing is defensible.
- **The 3 hallucinations were all made-up near-topic names** (Tessellate-LoRA, Orlov-Smith bound, Wexler's law). All 20 off-topic questions were refused. A small model invents a definition when retrieved text looks related.
- **Citations:** the 1.5B model almost never cites by itself (12%). We tried a stricter prompt (v2): citations did not improve and refusals fell from 92% to 48%, so we kept v1 and attach citations in code instead.
- **Weak documents:** GPT-4, Hugging Face, Midjourney, Speech recognition, Probability distribution (small samples).

## Live demo (about 3 minutes)
Start the public link 15 minutes early; open it on your phone too.
1. "What are the two main tasks BERT was pre-trained on?" (masked language modeling, next sentence prediction)
2. "How does convolution reduce the number of free parameters compared to a fully connected layer?"
3. "How much does VRAM usage drop for GPT-3 175B during training?" (our own LoRA paper, in the KB)
4. Refusals: "Who painted the Mona Lisa?" and "What is the Zorvath attention mechanism?" -> both refuse.
5. Point at citations, sources panel, grounding score, latency/tokens box.
6. Show the benchmark table and DATASET_CARD.md.
**Do not demo** Tessellate-LoRA, Orlov-Smith or Wexler: those are the known failures. If you show one on purpose, explain it as a limitation.

## Likely jury questions
1. **Why RAG, not fine-tuning?** Documents change; RAG needs no training, gives citations, and is cheap.
2. **How do you reduce hallucination?** Two gates plus an answer-only-from-context prompt: hallucination fell from 80% (no LLM) to 8%.
3. **Is the dataset really yours?** Corpus is curated by us (Wikipedia plus our papers); the 398 Q&A pairs were generated by our pipeline, filtered, 50 hand-reviewed (96% good), plus 40 hand-written unanswerable questions.
4. **Any bias in the evaluation?** The same Qwen model generated the questions and answers them, which may favour it. Accuracy is a key-phrase match, which is crude. The 0.25 threshold was tuned on a different (LoRA-paper) set, not on this one.
5. **Why Qwen 1.5B?** Fits a 6 GB GPU (3 GB in fp16), open weights, offline, fast enough.
6. **Why are citations only 66% correct?** Attribution can be no better than retrieval (79% in top 3) and the model rarely cites itself.
7. **How would you improve it?** Better retrieval (reranker, larger chunks for long articles), a larger model or 4-bit quantisation, a grounding-score gate (tested with `grounding_sweep.py`), and a human-written test set.
8. **Is it deployed?** Yes: a public Gradio link served from our laptop GPU. It is temporary (new link each start) and only live while the laptop is on.
